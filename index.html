Alcohol control, one Journey at a time

The only score you have to beat is the one you built yourself.

10Drinks is a quiet place to cut back or stop. Each Journey gives you 10 drinking days. You score as many sober days as you can before those 10 are used. Then a new Journey starts — and the goal is simple: beat your previous score.

How the score works

A Journey is like a match you play against 10 drinking days.

On a sober day, you score 1. On a drinking day, one of the 10 is used. The Journey ends when 10 drinking days are used — for example 12–10. Next Journey, beat that score. Strengthen the ability to resist, one match at a time.

A Journey is like a football match. On a sober day, you score 1 goal. On a drinking day, the opponent scores 1 goal. The match ends when 10 goals are scored against you — e.g. 12–10. The goal is simple: next match, beat your previous score — e.g. 15–10. Strengthen your ability to resist drinking, one match at a time.

01

You get 10 drinking days

Every Journey starts with the same allowance. Logging “I drank today” uses one of them. There is no hidden penalty and no shame meter — just a finite count.

02

Sober days are your score

Each day you log “I did not drink” adds 1 sober day. Keep going until the 10 drinking days are used. Your score is those sober days against 10 — 7–10, 15–10, 20–10.

03

Beat the score you earned

When a Journey ends, a new one starts with 10 drinking days again. Your Best Score is the personal mark to beat. You are not competing with anyone else.

Your first Journey

This will be your first Journey, so it will also be your best Journey. Once you complete it, your next goal is to beat this score in your second Journey. Your trajectory matters more than any single day.

Alcohol addiction is difficult to overcome, but not impossible. Your first score might be just 5 sober days against 10 drinking days. Later, you may reach 12. Don’t give up. Once you reach 20 sober days against 10 drinking days, you may find that you need less willpower to keep progressing further.

The in-app introduction uses another run of the same rule: 10–10, then 15–10, then 20–10, then 30–10. Goal: more sober days than your last journey.

How 10Drinks builds will-power

Every day has two choices.

Each journey has 10 drinking days. Log as many sober days as you can before 10 drinking days are used. Then this journey ends and a new one starts with 10 drinking days again.

That’s it. A Journey is your sober days scored against 10 drinking days. The dashboard keeps three numbers in view: the day you’re on, your Best Score, and the Current Journey — sober days and drinking days, written like 0–0 at the start.

Who it’s for

For people who feel stuck with alcohol and want a self-driven way back.

10Drinks is for anyone who wants to cut back or stop — whether you drink most days, binge on weekends, or have already tried to quit and slipped. You do not need a perfect first week. You need a score you can improve.

  • You want control, not a spectacle

    No leaderboards. No public streak shame. Your Best Score and your last Journey stay with you.

  • You can be honest about drinking days

    Logging a drinking day is not failure. It uses one of ten. Honesty is the win — a real score is one you can actually beat.

  • You care about the next match

    One hard day does not erase the Journey. Trajectory matters more than any single day.

Why it helps

Willpower is easier when the goal is personal and finite.

Open-ended “never drink again” can feel too large. A Journey is bounded: 10 drinking days, then you close the match and start again. You always know the score you are trying to beat — because you built it.

A previous self-built score

Your last Journey is the benchmark. Improvement is counted in sober days against the same 10 drinking days, so progress is readable: 7, then 10, then 13.

Logging honestly is the win

Each journey is a fresh chance to beat your Best Score. A real score is one you can actually beat. Logging a drinking day uses one of ten. It does not wipe the Journey.

Five ways a Journey can be a win

You win here in five ways. Any one of these is a victory. You don’t have to quit. You just have to be slightly better than yesterday.

  • Add one more sober day than last time.
  • Don’t increase your intake.
  • Lower your AUDIT-C score by even one point.
  • Beat your personal best.
  • Reach a milestone you haven’t hit before.

Knowledge

Short notes you can page through. A few of the ones in the app:

  • The WHO AUDIT-C is a 3-question clinical screening tool used by doctors in over 50 countries to identify hazardous and harmful drinking patterns. WHO AUDIT Guidelines 2001
  • Home pours are typically 40–60% larger than bar pegs. What feels like 2 drinks at home could be 3–4 standard drinks. The glass size is the illusion. NIAAA Drink Size Research
  • Needing more drinks to feel the same effect is not a badge of honour — it is a clinical warning sign. Tolerance means your liver and brain have adapted to alcohol as a baseline. NIAAA, DSM-5

Drinking Risk Meter

AUDIT-C, scored from 0 to 12, shows what your drinking level means.

AUDIT-C is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. A high score means professional assessment is recommended. Answers are kept for this session only and are not saved when you close the app.

  1. How often do you have a drink containing alcohol?

    Never · Monthly or less · 2 to 4 times a month · 2 to 3 times a week · 4 or more times a week

  2. How many standard drinks do you have on a typical drinking day?

    1 standard drink ≈ 30 ml of 40% whisky (about 10 g alcohol).

    • 1 or 2 drinks (30 or 60 ml)
    • 3 or 4 drinks (90 or 120 ml)
    • 5 or 6 drinks (~150–180 ml @ 40% whisky)
    • 7, 8 or 9 drinks (210 to 270 ml)
    • 10 or more drinks (300 ml+)

  3. How often do you have 6 or more drinks on one occasion?

    6 or more standard drinks is about 60 g of alcohol. 180 ml of 40% whisky contains about 57 g — just under 6 of the 10 g standard drinks used by AUDIT.

    Never · Less than monthly · Monthly · Weekly · Daily or almost daily

Reduction Goal

After the test, the app names one place to focus first.

  • Heavy occasions. Focus first: avoid heavy drinking occasions.
  • Amount. Focus first: reduce how much you drink per session.
  • Frequency. Focus first: drink on fewer days.
  • Lower-risk range. Less alcohol is always better. Maintaining or reducing further is worthwhile. Low risk is not risk free.

Alongside the focus, the app also suggests more alcohol-free days, smaller amounts, and fewer heavy occasions. Based on your screening answers. Not a personal medical recommendation.

Seek professional support

A score in the higher range means: consider speaking with a healthcare professional about your drinking.

If you drink heavily every day or have experienced withdrawal symptoms before, don’t stop or make a major reduction suddenly without medical guidance. Alcohol withdrawal can be dangerous and, in some cases, life-threatening. A healthcare professional can help you decide on a safe reduction or stopping plan.

Your log stays on this device.

  • Journey data stays in your browser storage. 10Drinks does not run accounts or upload your log to a server.
  • Drink-risk (AUDIT-C) answers are kept for this session only.
  • Export progress saves a backup file you control. Import progress restores from that file.
  • Reset all data clears journeys, streaks, and history on this device only. The app asks you to type RESET before it erases anything.
